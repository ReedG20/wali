Cover story — The pendant

Every day is a page. Mind reads it back to you.

A small pendant listens through your day, remembers the people and plans that make it up, and writes in the margins only when it matters.

Worn on a cord·Private by default·4 min read
Reserve a first edition Read how it works ↓

Your body has had its instruments for a while now. A ring counts your sleep and a strap counts your strain. Your mind, which is the part of you that actually lives the day, has had a notes app and good intentions.

Mind is a pendant you wear on a cord. With your permission it listens, and it turns the day into something you can read: who you saw, what you promised, and where your attention went.1

Over weeks it learns your shape. It knows the friend who leaves you lighter, the hour you lose to scrolling, and the book you keep meaning to finish.

Then it writes back, and not often. You might get one line at 11:48 p.m., or a drafted reply waiting for your yes.2 You might get a Saturday sidequest pulled from something you said in March. It doesn’t grade you. It keeps the record and remembers what you’d forget.

1Speech is transcribed on the pendant and your phone. Raw audio is deleted after 24 hours unless you keep it.

2Anything with weight, like money, messages to new people or public posts, waits for your approval.

§1

How it works

Three movements. The pendant hears the day, Mind writes it into a long memory, and it speaks up when a line is worth sending.

I.  The pendant

It hears the day, not just the steps.

It transcribes conversations with speaker labels, notices when you pick up your phone, and can briefly look when you ask. A press of the light pauses everything.

Specimen · Raw page, 9:14 a.m.
09:14PRIYACan we push Thursday? It’s a lot this week.
09:14YOUTotally. Let me look and get back to you.
II.  The memory

It remembers who and what matters.

Mind builds a private memory of your people, goals, habits and patterns. It knows Priya prefers mornings and that “get back to you” is a promise.

Specimen · Memory, updated

III.  The margin note

It speaks up, sparingly, and acts with your yes.

Nudges arrive as a tap and a line. Small, safe tasks it simply does. Anything with weight is drafted and waits for you.

§ 3  —  The terms of trust
You are the only reader.

A memoir only works if you can say anything in it. These terms are short, binding and written in plain English.

3.1Off is physical. Press the light and the microphones cut at the hardware. The light goes dark so the people around you know too.

3.2Audio doesn’t linger. Speech becomes text on your devices. Raw audio is gone in 24 hours unless you pin a moment.

3.3Other people are guests. Voices that aren’t yours are labelled, never profiled, and dropped from memory if they ask.

3.4Encrypted with your key. Your memory sits in a vault only you can open. We cannot read it, sell it or train on it.

3.5Actions have levels. You choose what Mind can do alone, what needs a yes, and what it never touches.

3.6Delete means delete. One page, one person or everything, gone in under a minute, with a receipt.
